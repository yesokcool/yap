# Why Yap exists

Discord got big. Yap is the part of it a friend group actually uses: **a place
to type, and a room to talk in.** That's the whole product.

## What it is
- Text channels and voice channels. Open the link, pick a name, you're in.
- Voice that sounds *better* than Discord's free tier, because the audio goes
  straight between friends — there's no server bill to ration it against.
- Light. A web page that loads instantly, native apps that sip memory. No
  Electron, ever.
- Free to run, forever, on hardware nobody has to keep switched on.

## What it refuses to become
- No servers-to-browse, no Nitro, no boosts, no profiles, no status flair,
  no presence. (An away message is a note you choose to leave; it only shows
  when someone mentions you or opens People.)
- No streaks, badges, "you've been away" nags, or engagement tricks of any
  kind. It's a tool; it should be quiet when you're not using it — and at
  night, with quiet hours, quieter still.
- No video calls, bots, or app directory. When the friend group genuinely
  misses something (screen sharing, Watch together, a soundboard), it comes
  in the smallest possible version.
- Nothing to manage: the invite link is the door, and an account is just a
  name and a PIN (a password only if you want one).

## Stance behind the defaults
- **Quality over thrift** where it's free: 128 kbps Opus by default, up to the
  codec's 510 kbps maximum.
- **Calm UI:** system fonts, quiet colors, light and dark, nothing that
  blinks for attention except a speaking ring.
- **Mistakes are recoverable:** archived channels and earlier versions of
  edited messages stay. The one exception is on purpose: a message you delete
  is erased (2026-10-05: "dont save deleted posts").
